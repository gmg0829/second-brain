## 演讲深度解析

### 引言：从高中辍学搬到 Tenderloin

Samir Vasavada 的开场极具冲击力：

> "I dropped out of high school and I moved to the Bay area, and I lived in this place called the tenderloin... But I didn't really give my parents a choice. My decision was, this is my passion. This is the thing that drives me. It was actually more than a passion. It was like a calling."

他明确区分了 passion（热情）和 calling（使命召唤）——对 Samir 来说，创业不只是"热门的事"，而是一种不得不然的内在驱动。

**关于风险的核心观点**：

> "The actual risk you're taking is not doing that right, because you're taking the risk of, am I going to be miserable in life if I don't actually follow my passion?"

他提出一个反直觉的论点：**不追随热情的风险，大于追随之的风险。** 特别是当一个人还年轻、没有家庭负担时，这是承担风险的黄金窗口期。

> "I'm 16, 17, 18 years old. Now is the time in my life where I should take the most risk because I have a safety net to fall back on."

### Chapter 1: 13岁的建设者

**起点：iPhone 时代的机遇**

Samir 回顾自己从小就热爱"建设东西"。iPhone 的发布和 Steve Jobs 的传记激发了他的技术创业热情。

他的父母是印度移民，在一个小城镇长大——"技术创始人"在他们看来是完全陌生的概念。

**第一个真正的创业机会——来自一次偶然的相遇**：

Samir 和他的联合创始人（后来的 Vise 联合创始人）在西北大学夏令营认识——两人宿舍门对门。

夏令营结束后，他们想："我们有大把时间，学校也不太难，不如赚点钱吧。"

当时 App 非常火热，每个小企业都想要一个 App。

**第一个创业想法：用 AI 自动生成 App**

> "What if we use AI to automate those apps? What if you could type in your app idea into a platform and we could build you an iPhone app in 30 seconds?"

这个想法对两个 13-14 岁的孩子来说太技术密集了。但他们的一位导师（前投资银行家）给了他们一个关键洞察：

> "You guys know so much about AI and machine learning now. You should consult with large investment banks because large investment banks want to learn about AI and machine learning for investment decisions and efficiency decisions."

他们开始为大型投资银行提供 AI/ML 咨询服务，收费 **$500-$1,000/小时**——这对两个青少年来说是惊人的副收入。

**关键洞察：Morgan Stanley 50% 的收入来自财富管理**

在咨询过程中，他们发现财富和资产管理是这些机构的核心收入来源。但金融顾问们虽然精通投资，却缺乏技术和工具来有效管理投资。

> "What if we build technology to help automate the investment workflows for these large investment institutions?"

**Jamie Dimon 的关键建议**

两人在底特律创业周冲上舞台，向 Jamie Dimon 提问是否想让 JP Morgan 用 AI 自动化投资顾问的工作流。

Dimon 直接拒绝了——但给了一个关键建议：

> "You should look at independent financial advisors, because what's happening is a lot of advisors are leaving big institutions and they're going independent. And you guys could provide the tech, the software to power them."

**这就是 Vise 发现的巨大市场：独立金融顾问。**

### Chapter 2: 从失败中学习

**Vise 的第一个原型**

他们在 15 岁时构建了第一个原型，目标卖给大型金融机构——但发现这极其困难。

**最艰难的日子：睡地板、被拒绝无数**

> "I had cold called, emailed hundreds of investors between the ages of 16 and 19, and no one would give us money while I was living in the Bay area, sleeping on floors."

联合创始人 Renick 决定先去宾大（U-Penn）读一年大学。Samir 的回应是：**搬到 Renick 的宿舍地板上住**，说："你不退学我就不走。"

**退学的条件：必须融资 100 万美元**

他们的父母说："如果你想退学或休学，你得先融到 100 万美元。"

**Sequoia 合伙人 Jim Goetz 的当面拒绝**

Samir 在网上找到 Jim Goetz（Sequoia 著名合伙人）的电话号码，打了过去。

> "I called him and I said, Jim, this is the business we're working on. But I was so nervous the first time I hung up."

Jim Goetz 在电话里骂了他 10 分钟，说："Sequoia 永远不会投资你们。"

讽刺的是，几年后 Sequoia 三次投资了 Vise。

**第一批真正的投资人：Flatiron Health 创始人**

在无数次拒绝后，他们遇到了 Nat Turner 和 Zach Weinberg（Flatiron Health 创始人）。

> "They said, okay, we'll be your first investors. Even if the thing goes to zero, it's okay. We think you'll be successful."

**$100K 的种子资金**，足以养活员工（当时所有人都在用股权工资工作），并给了他们继续前进的动力。

后来他们又拿到了 Founders Fund 领投的 **$200 万种子轮**。

**核心教训：你永远从失败中学习**

> "You never learn from your successes. You always learn from your failures. So our goal is to have as many failures as fast as possible."

**Reid Hoffman 原则的反面教训**

Samir 提到 Reid Hoffman 的一句名言："你的第一款产品应该让你感到尴尬。"

但 Vise 的问题是：**他们一直在 scrub（打磨）和 restart（重启），却从未发布。**

> "We were always embarrassed about our product, but we never actually launched it. If I had to redo it all over again, I would have given it to users, even though I was so desperately embarrassed about the product."

教训：**即便不完美，更多反馈才能持续迭代周期，让你不断学习和进步。**

**每天都坐在金融顾问的办公室里**

Samir 分享了他早期最重要的学习方式：

> "Every day after school, I would sit in financial advisors offices and I would just watch them use our product or watch them use other product. And then it builds a really strong mental model and understanding of how do they think, how do they make decisions."

**YC 的原则永远正确：Always be talking to your customers.**

### Chapter 3: 对的人做出更好的结果

**硅谷最大的误区**

> "There is a belief in Silicon Valley that more people and more capital builds businesses faster, and that's actually a fallacy. The right people that are thinking about solving the right customer problems, operating at the right speed, is what makes companies grow and scale faster."

**不是更多人和更多钱——是对的人解决对的问题，以对的速度。**

**Top 20% 驱动 Top 80% 的结果**

> "The top 20% of performers drive the top 80% of results, so why do you want the other 80%? You want only the top performers at the company."

**常春藤的迷失**

Samir 反思了自己曾经相信的"名校=聪明人"的假设：

> "I used to believe the smartest people, they went to the best universities, they went to Harvard, they went to Yale, they went to Stanford. That's actually not true. And often when we've hired those people, we've actually been disappointed and we've been wrong."

**Vise 招聘的教训：招募了错误的人**

Vise 在 18 个月内从种子轮到 $10 亿估值——创下了历史最快纪录。但他们随之招募了**错误的人**：

> "We recruited people that were here to get rich. They were mercenaries. They wanted to join Vise and expected it to be a rocket ship, and expected them not to have to solve problems, when in reality we were still an early stage startup."

结果：他们花了很大力气让这些人离开，重建内部文化。

**SKQ 招聘框架**

Samir 提出了自己总结的招聘框架：

| 维度 | 描述 | 重要性 |
|---|---|---|
| **Skills（技能）** | 完成工作所需的具体能力 | 可培养 |
| **Knowledge（知识）** | 对特定领域的了解深度 | 可获取 |
| **Qualities（品质）** | 愿意超级努力工作？能学习？灵活？能接受反馈？有驱动力？ | **最重要，不可培养** |

**Vise 三条核心价值观**

1. **Burn the Boats（烧船心态）**
   - 全身心投入使命，愿意冲破任何墙壁
   - "We're going to figure it out" 的心态

2. **1% Better Every Day**
   - 每天进步 1%，一年后你会强大很多
   - 小的变化 + 持续复合 = 指数级成长

3. **Customer Obsession（客户痴迷）**
   - 每个决策都思考：客户受到什么影响？我们如何为客户创造更多价值？
   - 即便短期有个人代价，也要把客户放在首位

### Chapter 4: 做你热爱的事

**最艰难的时期**

Samir 回顾了最艰难的创业时刻：

> "I think I've had times where we were at the top of the world, and I was feeling kind of lonely and misunderstood and just miserable, and I was like, feeding for scraps, basically. I would like go to the Whole Foods and steal from the buffet because I had no money and I needed food."

在最辉煌的时刻，他感到孤独、被误解、一文不名——只能去 Whole Foods 自助餐偷吃。

但他说：

> "But it was also really important because one, it built grit and two, it built this sense of drive and purpose that if I didn't figure it out, I was kind of screwed."

**烧船心态——没有退路，才能全力以赴。**

**给创业者的忠告**

Samir 的建议极其真诚：

> "I don't recommend starting a company at a young age unless it is your true driving passion. I think a lot of people think, wow, it's really cool to start a company... You should do the thing that you're passionate about, that you're excited about."

**如果你真的热爱，创业是理所当然的选择——你会学到比你想象的更多。**

**成功的定义：建造全球最大的资产管理公司**

Samir 的愿景：

> "My vision is to build the world's largest asset manager, entirely powered by technology. How do we transform this legacy archaic age old industry into the modern era?"

> "As long as you keep going, you will eventually get there."
