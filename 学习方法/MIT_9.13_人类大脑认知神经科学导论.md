## Part 1: Opening Story — Bob's Brain (Core Case Study)
## 第一部分：开场故事 —— Bob的大脑（核心案例研究）

### The Incident / 事件经过

Nancy's friend "Bob" was staying overnight en route to a conference in Cambridge. He was a close friend — they'd known each other for years, hiked together, chatted regularly. The plan was for him to leave early the next morning.

Nancy的友人"Bob"当时正要去参加一个会议，借住在Nancy位于剑桥的家中过夜。他们是多年好友，一起徒步旅行，经常聊天。原本计划是第二天一早就出发。

Early that morning, Nancy heard shuffling, then a crash. She found Bob lying on the floor, unresponsive. She shouted his name — no answer. She called 911. While waiting, Bob started to wake up, very woozy, making some sense but unable to figure out what was happening.

那天一大早，Nancy听到窸窣声，然后是一声巨响。她发现Bob倒在地板上，没有反应。她喊他的名字——没有回应。她拨打了911。在等待救护车时，Bob开始醒来，但非常虚弱，能说一些话但搞不清状况。

EMTs arrived in about 3 minutes, took vitals — found nothing wrong. They offered to take him to the ER or go on his own. Nancy insisted on the ER at Mount Auburn Hospital (less than a mile away). After hours of tests and questions, doctors couldn't find anything. Bob seemed fine. Nancy left for work, telling Bob to text her.

急救人员约3分钟到达，测量了生命体征——一切正常。他们提议可以送他去医院或他自己去。Nancy坚持让他去急诊室（不到一英里外的Mount Auburn医院）。经过数小时的检查和询问，医生找不出任何问题。Bob看起来没事了。Nancy去上班，告诉Bob给她发短信。

### The Hidden Warning Signs / 被忽视的预警信号

But as Nancy was leaving, a thought flashed through her mind: **"You should check his brain."** The reason: Bob had been showing strange signs for years — he often got lost and didn't know where he was. Yet in every other way, he was brilliant: holding down a high-powered job, writing beautiful prose, the life of every party, witty and funny.

但就在Nancy离开时，一个念头闪过：**"你应该检查他的大脑。"** 原因是：Bob多年来一直表现出奇怪的迹象——他经常迷路，不知道自己在哪。然而在其他所有方面，他都非常出色：担任高职、写出优美的文章、是每个聚会的焦点、聪明幽默。

Examples / 例子：

- A friend asked Bob how to drive from his house to Cambridge. He said "turn left at the end of the driveway." It was actually a right turn.
- 朋友问Bob从他家开车到剑桥怎么走，他说"在车道尽头左转"。实际上应该是右转。

- Bob didn't know how to get to the grocery store in his own hometown — a trip he'd made hundreds of times.
- Bob不知道在自己住了很久的家乡怎么去杂货店——那是他去过数百次的地方。

- In Germany, Nancy saw a poster: "Navigational deficits: an early sign of Alzheimer's." She suppressed the thought, not wanting to face it.
- 在德国时，Nancy看到一张海报："导航缺陷：阿尔茨海默病的早期征兆。"她压抑了这个想法，不想面对。

**The irony / 讽刺的是**: Nancy's own research for 20 years had proven exactly this — that different parts of the brain do different things, so you could lose one ability while keeping others. Yet she didn't connect the dots for her own friend.

**讽刺的是**：Nancy自己20年的研究恰恰证明了这一点——大脑的不同部分做不同的事情，所以你可以失去一种能力而保留其他能力。但她却没有为自己朋友的情况做出这个联想。

### The Diagnosis / 诊断结果

That night, Bob called: "They found something in my brain." At the ER, Nancy saw the scan — **a lime-sized tumor** right next to the parahippocampal place area (PPA), which her lab had discovered nearly 20 years earlier with postdoc Russell Epstein. The PPA is specifically involved in navigation and scene recognition.

那天晚上，Bob打来电话："他们在我大脑里发现了东西。"在急诊室，Nancy看到了扫描图——**一颗柠檬大小的肿瘤**，正好位于海马旁位置区（PPA）旁边，这是她的实验室近20年前与博士后Russell Epstein一起发现的。PPA专门负责导航和场景识别。

Even more poignant: Nancy had brain scans of Bob from years earlier, when he'd participated in an experiment in her lab. Those scans showed the same spot — but **the size of a grape**. It had been growing slowly, which meant it was likely a meningioma (not cancer) — bad but not the worst kind.

更令人感慨的是：Nancy有Bob多年以前在她实验室参与实验时的大脑扫描。那些扫描显示同样位置——但**只有葡萄大小**。它一直在缓慢生长，这意味着这可能是脑膜瘤（不是癌症）——不好，但不是最恶性的一种。

### The Surgery / 手术

The surgery was extreme: 11 hours, removing a huge piece of bone from the back of the head, separating the brain hemispheres, going in multiple inches. The tumor was wrapped around the **vein of Galen** — a massive vein even Galen (2,000 years ago) could find. Because they found the best neurosurgeon (privileged access), Bob sailed through. An hour after surgery, he was chatting. Two days later, home. A few days after that, back at work.

手术非常极端：11小时，从后脑勺取下一大块骨头，分开大脑半球，深入数英寸。肿瘤缠绕在**盖伦静脉**——一条巨大的静脉，即使2000年前的盖伦也能找到。由于找到了最好的神经外科医生（得益于特权资源），Bob手术很顺利。术后一小时他在聊天。两天后出院。几天后回去上班。

**But his navigational abilities? None of them came back at all.**
**但他的导航能力呢？完全没有恢复。**

### The Bedside Neuropsychological Testing / 床旁神经心理学测试

The night before surgery, Nancy tested Bob:

术前那晚，Nancy对Bob进行了测试：

| Test / 测试 | Result / 结果 | What It Means / 含义 |
|------------|--------------|---------------------|
| Draw floor plan of his house / 画他家的平面图 | Rooms not aligned, soup of lines, no organization / 房间没对齐，线条混乱，没有组织 | Can't visualize/spatial layout impaired / 无法可视化/空间布局能力受损 |
| Draw floor plan of Nancy's house / 画Nancy家的平面图 | Same mess / 同样一团糟 | Not just unfamiliarity / 不仅仅是不熟悉 |
| Draw a bicycle / 画一辆自行车 | Recognizable, all parts in right spatial relation / 可辨认，所有部件空间关系正确 | Object parts memory INTACT / 物体部件记忆完整 |
| Draw a lobster / 画一只龙虾 | Also recognizable / 同样可辨认 | Not about complex multi-part objects / 不是关于复杂多部件物体 |

**Key insight / 关键洞察**: His deficit is **specifically about spatial relationships in places/environments**, not about objects. Drawing a bicycle requires remembering parts and their relationships, but in a different way than navigating a house.

**关键洞察**：他的缺陷**专门针对场所/环境中的空间关系**，而不是物体。画自行车需要记忆部件及其关系，但方式不同于在房子里导航。

### What Bob Can and Cannot Do Now / Bob现在能做什么和不能做什么

**Intact abilities / 完整保留的能力**:
- Right/left distinction / 完全辨别左右
- General intelligence / 总体智力
- Object recognition / 物体识别
- Face recognition / 人脸识别
- Distance estimation / 距离判断
- Retracing steps briefly / 短暂折返
- Memorized sequences (old routes: A→B→C→D) / 记忆的路线序列
- Speaking, writing, wit, humor / 说话、写作、智慧、幽默

**Lost abilities / 完全丧失的能力**:
- Navigation in new/unfamiliar places / 在新地方/不熟悉的地方导航
- Devising alternate routes when blocked / 规划替代路线
- Visualizing nearby places / 想象附近的地方
- Indoor navigation (new hotels, buildings) / 室内导航（新酒店、建筑物）
- Constructing stable mental images of spatial layouts / 构建稳定的空间布局心理图像

**Bob's own description / Bob对自己体验的描述**:

When looking at a place, he knows where he is (lots of other cues: intention, context). It looks familiar. But if he tries to imagine what's behind him — "it just vaporizes." He can't construct or hold a stable mental image of nearby places.

当看着一个地方时，他知道自己在哪（有大量其他线索：意图、背景）。看起来很熟悉。但如果他试着想象身后是什么——"它就蒸发了。"他无法构建或保持对附近地方的稳定心理图像。

**"Thank God for iPhones. If Bob lived 30 years ago, he wouldn't be able to function."**
**"感谢上帝有iPhone。如果Bob生活在30年前，他将无法正常生活。"**

## Part 2: Five Major Themes of the Course
## 第二部分：课程的五个核心主题

### Theme 1: Brain Has Structure and Organization — Not a Homogeneous Mass
### 主题1：大脑有结构和组织——不是均匀一团

The brain isn't just a "big bunch of mush." Different parts do different things. When Bob had a lime-sized tumor, he didn't get "a little stupid" — his IQ would have been unchanged. He lost a **very specific mental ability**. This organization is what makes neuroscience tractable: if there's structure, there's a place to start understanding.

大脑不是"一团糊状物"。不同部分做不同的事情。当Bob有一颗柠檬大小的肿瘤时，他没有变得"有点傻"——他的智商不会改变。他失去的是**一种非常具体的心理能力**。这种组织性使得神经科学变得可把握：如果有结构，就有了理解的起点。

### Theme 2: Some Brain Parts Do Extremely Specific Things
### 主题2：某些脑区做极其专门化的事情

Not all brain regions are general-purpose. Some are remarkably specific:
并非所有脑区都是通用型的。有些非常专门化：

- **Parahippocampal Place Area (PPA)**: Responds selectively to scenes, not faces, objects, or words
- **海马旁位置区（PPA）**：专门对场景响应，而不是人脸、物体或文字
- **Fusiform Face Area (FFA)**: Responds selectively to faces
- **梭状回面孔区（FFA）**：专门对人脸响应

This specificity is what makes the field progress — when you find a brain region that does X and not Y, you can study X in isolation.

这种专门性推动了该领域的进步——当你发现一个脑区做X而不做Y时，你可以单独研究X。

### Theme 3: Brain Organization Echoes Mind Architecture
### 主题3：大脑组织反映心灵架构

The fundamental pieces of the brain tell us the fundamental parts of the mind. The brain is just physical cells — but studying it tells us about the mind. The real question isn't "where in the brain" — it's "which mental processes have their own specialized machinery and **why those**?"

大脑的基本部件告诉我们心灵的基本部分。大脑只是物理细胞——但研究它能让我们了解心灵。真正的问题不是"在大脑的哪里"——而是"哪些心理过程有自己的专门硬件，为什么是这些？"

### Theme 4: How Do Brains Change?
### 主题4：大脑如何变化？

- Normal development / 正常发育
- Learning and experience / 学习和经验
- After injury / 损伤后
- **Critical difference**: Children with early brain damage often recover. Adults — not so much. Bob didn't recover his navigational abilities because the damage happened in adulthood.
- **关键差异**：儿童早期脑损伤往往能恢复。成人——恢复困难得多。Bob没有恢复导航能力，因为损伤发生在成年后。

### Theme 5: Many Ways to Study the Brain
### 主题5：研究大脑的多种方法

| Method / 方法 | What It Tells You / 它告诉你的信息 |
|--------------|----------------------------------|
| Behavioral observations / 行为观察 | Which mental abilities preserved/lost / 哪些心理能力保留/丧失 |
| Anatomical brain images / 解剖脑图像 | Where is physical damage / 物理损伤在哪里 |
| Functional MRI (fMRI) / 功能磁共振 | Which brain regions active during tasks / 任务期间哪些脑区活跃 |
| Neuropsychology patients / 神经心理学患者 | Causal brain-behavior relationships / 因果的脑-行为关系 |
| EEG/MEG | Real-time brain activity dynamics / 实时脑活动动态 |
| Diffusion tractography / 扩散张量成像 | Brain connectivity / 脑连接 |
| Single-neuron recordings / 单神经元记录 | Fine-grained neural computation / 精细神经计算 |

## Part 3: Why Study the Human Brain?
## 第三部分：为什么要研究人类大脑？

### Reason 1: Know Thyself / 理由1：认识自我

This is who you are — your brain. Hearts, livers, kidneys are important but they're not your identity. That's why surgeons do heart transplants but not brain transplants — if something's wrong with your brain, putting someone else's brain in wouldn't make sense because **the brain is who you are**.

这就是你——你的大脑。心脏、肝脏、肾脏很重要，但它们不是你的身份。这就是为什么外科医生做心脏移植而不做脑移植——如果你大脑有问题，把别人的大脑放进去没有意义，因为**大脑就是你**。

That's why it was the "Decade of the Brain" — not the decade of the pancreas or liver.

这就是为什么是"脑的十年"——而不是胰腺或肝脏的十年。

### Reason 2: Understand Limits of Human Knowledge
### 理由2：理解人类知识的极限

Studying the mind is a form of **empirical epistemology** — studying the knower to understand the nature of knowledge. Are there scientific theories we can never understand? Thoughts we can't think? The human mind has limits, and studying it tells us what those limits are.

研究心灵是一种**经验认识论**——研究认识者以理解知识的本质。是否存在我们永远无法理解的科学理论？无法思考的思想？人类心灵有极限，研究它告诉我们这些极限是什么。

### Reason 3: Advance AI / 理由3：推进人工智能

Until 2012, machines were far behind human vision. Then came **AlexNet** (Krizhevsky et al.), trained on ImageNet — a revolution with 33,000+ citations. Deep nets now approach human-level object recognition.

直到2012年，机器在视觉上还远落后于人类。然后是**AlexNet**（Krizhevsky等），在ImageNet上训练——一场革命，33,000+引用。深度网络现在接近人类水平的物体识别。

**But they're not there yet / 但它们还没到达那个水平**:

On ImageNet: 71% correct. On realistic, varied images with atypical viewpoints: **25% correct**. Deep nets fail at:
在ImageNet上：71%正确。在逼真的、多样的、非典型视角的图片上：**25%正确**。深度网络的短板：

- Atypical object positions/angles / 不典型的物体位置/角度
- Non-canonical examples / 非规范样本
- Image understanding beyond classification / 超越分类的图像理解

**Image captioning failures** — "A dinosaur on top of a surfboard" is good. But a picture of Obama putting his foot on a scale while Comey looks on? "A group of people standing next to a man in a suit and tie." **Missing the entire point of the joke.**

**图像描述的失败**——"冲浪板上的恐龙"还不错。但奥巴马在称重时把脚放在秤上而科米在旁边看着的图片？描述为"一群人站在一个穿西装打领带的男人旁边"。**完全错过了笑话的要点。**

**What AI still can't do** (Jim DiCarlo, head of MIT Brain & Cognitive Sciences):
**AI仍然做不到的事情**（麻省理工学院脑与认知科学系主任Jim DiCarlo）：

- Navigate new situations / 在新情况下导航
- Infer what others believe / 推断他人信念
- Use language to communicate / 用语言交流
- Write poetry/music expressing feelings / 写表达情感的诗歌/音乐
- Create math to build bridges, devices, life-saving medicines / 创造数学来建造桥梁、设备、救命药物

**The key distinction / 关键区别**: Both humans and deep nets are good at pattern recognition/classification. But humans excel at **building models to understand the world** — rich structural understanding, social context, causality, humor.

**关键区别**：人类和深度网络都擅长模式识别/分类。但人类擅长**构建模型来理解世界**——丰富的结构性理解、社会背景、因果关系、幽默。

> "AI is taking a massive leap now and we're right in the middle of it. But AI has a lot to learn from us too."
> "AI正在大步前进，我们正身处其中。但AI也有很多要向我们学习的。"

### Reason 4: The Greatest Intellectual Quest of All Time
### 理由4：有史以来最伟大的智力探索

Nancy believes this is more profound than cosmology. Deep questions everyone wonders about:
Nancy认为这比宇宙学更深刻。每个人都想知道深度问题：

- How come we can study this thing that studies itself? / 我们怎么能研究这个能研究自身的东西？
- How much of knowledge is innate vs. learned? / 多少知识是天生的vs.习得的？
- Can we grow new brain regions for new abilities? / 我们能为新能力长出新的脑区吗？
- Can we think without language? / 我们能不用语言思考吗？
- Can we perceive without awareness? / 我们能在没有意识的情况下感知吗？

There are **empirical answers** to these long-standing questions. That's extraordinary.
这些长期存在的问题有**经验性的答案**。这很了不起。

## Part 4: How to Study the Brain — The Course Approach
## 第四部分：如何研究大脑——课程方法

### The Central Question / 核心问题

**How does the brain give rise to the mind?**
**大脑如何产生心灵？**

Approach / 方法:

1. Start with the **mind** — understand mental functions
   从**心灵**开始——理解心理功能
2. Ask: Is there **specialized machinery** for this function?
   问：这项功能是否有**专门的硬件**？
3. Ask: What information is represented, when, and how?
   问：什么信息被表征，何时表征，如何表征？

### Key Methods / 关键方法

- **Psychophysics / 感知心理物理学**: Visual/auditory stimuli → judgments (reaction time, accuracy) — 100+ years old, still powerful / 视觉/听觉刺激→判断（反应时、准确率）——100多年历史，仍然强大
- **Perceptual illusions / 知觉错觉**: Reveal how minds work / 揭示心灵如何运作
- **Neuropsychology / 神经心理学**: Patients with brain damage — oldest method in the field, still hugely important / 脑损伤患者——领域中最古老的方法，仍然非常重要
- **fMRI / 功能磁共振**: Which brain regions are active during tasks / 任务期间哪些脑区活跃
- **Neurophysiology / 神经生理学**: Single-neuron recordings in animals (and rare human cases) / 动物单神经元记录（偶尔人类案例）
- **EEG/MEG / 脑电图/脑磁图**: Real-time scalp brain activity / 实时头皮脑活动
- **Diffusion tractography / 扩散张量成像**: Brain connectivity / 脑连接

## Part 5: The Field's Massive Progress — 1990 vs. Now
## 第五部分：该领域的巨大进展——1990年 vs 现在

### 1990 State of Knowledge / 1990年的知识状态

What was known / 已知内容：

- Primary sensory/motor regions (known for decades) / 初级感觉/运动区（已知数十年）
- Face recognition suspected in right posterior hemisphere (from brain damage patients) / 面部识别怀疑在右后半球（来自脑损伤患者）
- Language regions from Broca and Wernicke (~200 years of knowledge) / 布洛卡区和韦尼克区的语言区（近200年知识）
- Attention direction in parietal lobes / 顶叶注意方向

**That's about it. Almost nothing else about high-level cognition.**
**大概就这些。关于高级认知几乎一无所知。**

### 2019 State of Knowledge / 2019年的知识状态

**Dozens of brain regions** with well-understood functions, identified in every person, thanks largely to fMRI. Major progress in:
**数十个脑区**功能被很好地理解，在每个人身上都能识别，主要归功于fMRI。在以下方面取得重大进展：

- Visual perception (color, shape, motion) / 视觉感知（颜色、形状、运动）
- Face recognition / 面部识别
- Place/scene recognition / 地点/场景识别
- Body recognition / 身体识别
- Word recognition / 文字识别
- Number processing / 数字加工
- Speech perception / 言语知觉
- Music perception / 音乐知觉
- Language understanding / 语言理解
- Theory of mind (thinking about others) / 心理理论（思考他人）
- Navigation / 导航

### Paper Reading Strategy / 论文阅读策略

When reading a scientific paper, ask:
阅读科学论文时，问：

1. **What question is being asked?** Look in the abstract. / **要回答什么问题？** 看摘要。
2. **What did they find?** Also in the abstract. / **他们发现了什么？** 也在摘要里。
3. **What's the interpretation / why does it matter?** The "who cares" question. / **如何解释/为什么重要？** "谁在乎"的问题。
4. **What was the experimental design?** Often in methods section. / **实验设计是什么？** 通常在方法部分。
5. **How were the data analyzed?** / **数据如何分析？**
6. **What's the gobbledygook you can safely ignore?** (scanning details, specific parameter settings) / **哪些冗余内容可以安全忽略？**（扫描细节、特定参数设置）

> "Understanding a scientific paper in your own area takes hours. It's only 3 pages — but you can't do it in 20 minutes."
> "在自己的领域理解一篇科学论文需要数小时。它只有3页——但你不可能20分钟搞定。"

- Field moves too fast / 领域发展太快

- Reading **hot-off-the-press original research papers** is the point / 阅读**新鲜出炉的原创研究论文**才是重点
- Teach students to read real papers, not secondhand summaries / 教学生读真正的论文，而不是二手摘要

## Key Conceptual Framework: The Nancy Kanwisher Method
## 关键概念框架：Nancy Kanwisher方法论

```
Mental Ability / 心理能力
     ↓
Tease apart: Is it exactly this or exactly that?
分解：它究竟是什么？
     ↓
Are these things separable? Can you lose one and not the other?
这些东西可分离吗？你能只失去一个而保留另一个吗？
     ↓
Do they live in different parts of the brain?
它们位于大脑的不同部位吗？
     ↓
What does that tell us about the architecture of the mind?
这告诉我们关于心灵的架构什么？
```

**The fundamental insight / 根本洞察**:

The brain has parts that do different things. By studying what specific mental abilities are preserved vs. lost (in patients like Bob), and where in the brain those abilities are implemented (fMRI), we can map the architecture of the mind.

大脑有不同功能的部分。通过研究哪些具体心理能力保留vs.丧失（在Bob这样的患者中），以及这些能力在大脑中实现的位置（fMRI），我们可以绘制心灵的架构图。

## Appendix: The Discovery of the PPA
## 附录：PPA的发现

The PPA was discovered nearly 20 years before Bob's diagnosis — by accident. Postdoc Russell Epstein was a computer vision researcher who wanted to understand how we see by writing code to duplicate visual algorithms. He pooh-poohed brain imaging: "The flash in the pan, it's going to go by, it's trashy. So you guys get nice blobs on the brain. I'm not having any of it."

PPA是在Bob确诊近20年前被偶然发现的。博士后Russell Epstein是一位计算机视觉研究者，想通过编写代码复制视觉算法来理解我们如何看东西。他不看好脑成像："昙花一现，会过去的，是垃圾。所以你们在大脑上得到一些漂亮的blob。我才不稀罕。"

Nancy convinced him to do "one dumb experiment" for his job talk — scan people looking at pictures of scenes vs. other pictures. The results "knocked our socks off": a brain region responded very selectively to scenes, not to faces, objects, or words. They called it the **parahippocampal place area**. It launched a major line of work in Nancy's lab and dozens of labs worldwide.

Nancy说服他做"一个笨实验"来充实他的学术报告——扫描人们看场景图片vs.其他图片时的脑区活动。结果"令人震惊"：一个脑区对场景有很高的选择性反应，而对人脸、物体或文字没有。他们称之为**海马旁位置区**。这在Nancy的实验室和全球数十个实验室开启了重要研究方向。
