### AI for Low-Pesticide Agriculture人工智能助力低农药农业

By [Garry Tan](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/garry-tan) 作者： [Garry Tan](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/garry-tan)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uwe-5PB9p4Y)

Modern agriculture runs on chemicals. That worked for a while. But now the cracks are obvious. Pesticide residues are everywhere: in food, in water, in soil. People worldwide are worrying about long-term health risks with glyphosate. At the same time, nature is adapting. Weeds and pests evolve. What used to work stops working. So farmers spray more. Costs go up. Margins go down. The pipeline for new chemicals? Slower and more expensive than ever. Farmers are stuck in a bad loop: Use more chemicals → get diminishing results → pay more → take on more risk. And yet they can't just stop. If pests win, crops die. Food prices go up. People might starve. It's existential. For a long time, this looked like an unsolvable problem. Now it doesn't. A few things changed all at once: AI can now see. It can identify individual weeds and pests in real time. Sensors and cameras got cheap enough to put everywhere. Robotics can act with precision, treating one plant instead of blanketing an entire field. And biology is catching up too: Microbes, peptides, RNA-based solutions; these aren't science fiction anymore. They can replace entire classes of synthetic chemicals. We can engineer plants to defend themselves. To outcompete weeds. To need less external input. And of course, so is AI. Real science breakthroughs will be augmented by AGI, and now's the time to see it. This is a massive shift. Agriculture is one of the biggest markets in the world. If you can lower costs and increase yields at the same time, adoption isn't slow. It's explosive. The company that cuts pesticide use by 90% **and** helps farmers grow more food? That's not just a good business. That's a generational company. If you're working on this, if you're building tools that help farmers produce more with fewer chemicals, we want to talk.  
现代农业依赖化学品。 这种方法起初奏效，但现在弊端显而易见。农药残留无处不在：食物中、水中、土壤里。全世界的人们都在担忧草甘膦带来的长期健康风险。 与此同时，大自然也在不断适应。杂草和害虫也在进化。过去有效的防治方法不再奏效。于是，农民不得不增加喷洒农药的次数。成本上升，利润下降。而新化学品的研发进程呢？比以往任何时候都更加缓慢，成本也更高。 农民们陷入了一个恶性循环：使用更多化学品→收效甚微→成本更高→承担更大风险。然而，他们却无法停止。如果害虫肆虐，庄稼就会枯死，食品价格上涨，人们甚至可能挨饿。这关乎生死存亡。 长期以来，这似乎是一个无法解决的问题。但现在并非如此。 几件事同时发生了变化： 人工智能现在有了视觉能力。它可以实时识别杂草和害虫。 传感器和摄像头价格已经非常便宜，可以随处放置。 机器人可以精准操作，单独处理一株植物，而不是覆盖整片田地。 生物学也在迎头赶上： 微生物、肽、RNA 解决方案；这些不再是科幻小说里的情节。它们可以取代整类合成化学物质。我们可以改造植物，使其能够自我防御，战胜杂草，减少对外部投入的需求。当然，人工智能也是如此。真正的科学突破将借助通用人工智能（AGI）的力量，而现在正是见证这一切的时刻。 这是一次巨大的转变。农业是世界上最大的市场之一。如果能够同时降低成本并提高产量，那么技术的普及速度不会缓慢，而是会呈爆炸式增长。 这家公司能将农药使用量减少 90% **，并** 帮助农民种植更多粮食？ 那不仅仅是一家优秀的企业，那是一家可以传承几代的公司。 如果你正在从事这方面的工作，如果你正在开发能够帮助农民用更少的化学品生产更多粮食的工具，我们想和你谈谈。

### AI-Native Service CompaniesAI 原生服务公司

By [Gustaf Alströmer](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/gustaf-alstromer)  
作者： [古斯塔夫·阿尔斯特罗](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/gustaf-alstromer) 默

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lsRnwM_ZdJY)

AI models are improving really fast, and they're now able to do complex work far beyond engineering. Historically, services became SaaS software. More recently, they became AI copilots. That's what most startups from 2023 to 2025 built: tools that help people do their jobs. What we're excited about now is the next step: AI-native companies that don't sell software—they sell the service. Instead of giving you a tool, they just do the work. The reason this matters is simple. The total spend on services is many times larger than the spend on software. And a lot of these services are already outsourced, which makes them much easier to replace with an AI-native product. We're especially interested in areas like: - Insurance brokerage - Accounting, tax, and audit - Compliance - Healthcare administration If you're building a company that replaces a service—not just improves it—we'd love to hear from you.  
人工智能模型发展迅速，现在能够完成远超工程领域的复杂工作。 从历史上看，服务演变成了 SaaS 软件。而最近，它们又演变成了人工智能助手。这正是 2023 年至 2025 年间大多数初创公司所打造的：帮助人们完成工作的工具。 我们现在最兴奋的是下一步： 有些人工智能原生公司不卖软件，而是卖服务。他们不给你工具，而是帮你完成工作。 这件事之所以重要，原因很简单。服务方面的总支出远高于软件支出。而且，很多服务已经外包，这使得用人工智能原生产品替代它们要容易得多。 我们尤其对以下领域感兴趣： - 保险经纪 会计、税务和审计 - 遵守 医疗保健管理 如果你正在打造一家能够取代现有服务（而不仅仅是改进现有服务）的公司，我们非常希望听到你的消息。

### AI Personalized Medicine人工智能个性化医疗

By [Ankit Gupta](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/ankit-gupta)  
作者： [安基特·古普塔](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/ankit-gupta)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fzVuQ2sYEn8)

Intelligent agents are enabling a new level of personalization in medical care. We can now use an agent harness like Claude Code to analyze personalized health data, whether that be a diagnostic test, genome scan, EHR data, or wearables information to get highly accurate, user-specific suggestions. At the same time, two big revolutions in science are occurring. First, the cost of generating personalized diagnostics is plummeting. The cost of genome sequencing has fallen at a rate faster than Moore's law, and a variety of new diagnostics are entering the market enabling early detection of a variety of health signals. Second, the cost of printing n of 1 genetic therapies is plummeting. We can now design and deliver personalized medicines through delivery vectors like mRNA, and the FDA has expressed more openness to letting patients try out these procedures. We think these factors collectively are going to bring about a revolution in care delivery. Abundant data and intelligence can help patients more accurately assess their disease risk and democratize access to treatments for the most serious illnesses. To enable this, we believe a wide variety of new startups will support every step of this ecosystem. If you're building for the future of intelligent personalized care, we'd love to hear from you.  
智能代理正在将医疗保健的个性化提升到一个全新的水平。我们现在可以使用像 Claude Code 这样的代理工具来分析个性化的健康数据，无论是诊断测试、基因组扫描、电子病历数据还是可穿戴设备信息，从而获得高度精准且针对用户的建议。 与此同时，科学领域正在发生两场重大革命。 首先，个性化诊断的成本正在急剧下降。基因组测序的成本下降速度甚至超过了摩尔定律，而且各种新型诊断技术正不断涌入市场，能够早期检测多种健康信号。 其次，单剂量基因疗法的成本正在急剧下降。我们现在可以通过 mRNA 等递送载体设计和递送个性化药物，而且 FDA 也表示更愿意让患者尝试这些疗法。 我们认为这些因素加在一起将会给医疗服务带来一场革命。 丰富的数据和信息可以帮助患者更准确地评估患病风险，并使更多人能够获得针对最严重疾病的治疗。 为了实现这一目标，我们相信各种各样的新兴创业公司将为这一生态系统的每个环节提供支持。如果您正在为未来智能个性化医疗的发展而努力，我们非常期待与您交流。

### Company Brain公司大脑

By [Tom Blomfield](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/tom-blomfield)  
作者： [汤姆·布隆](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/tom-blomfield) 菲尔德

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IaWIazkWWog)

The biggest blocker to AI automation of companies is no longer the models, they just got so good so quickly. Now the blocker is the domain knowledge. Every company has critical know-how scattered everywhere. Some of it lives in people's heads. Some of it is buried in old email accounts, Slack threads, support tickets, and databases. The company works because humans vaguely remember where that knowledge is and how to apply it. But AI agents can't operate like that. If we want every company to run on AI automation, we need a new primitive: a company brain. We need Garry's G-Brain, but for every business in the world. A system that pulls knowledge out of all these fragmented sources, structures it, keeps it current, and turns it into an executable skills file for AI. This isn't a company-wide search or a chatbot over documents. It's a living map of how a company works: how refunds get handled, how pricing exceptions are decided or how engineers respond to incidents. Then AI systems can use that skills file to actually do the work safely and consistently. The company brain becomes the missing layer between raw company data and reliable AI automation. I think every company in the world is going to need one. If you're building this, you should apply to YC.  
企业实现人工智能自动化的最大障碍不再是模型本身，因为模型发展得太快太出色了。现在的障碍是领域知识。 每家公司都拥有分散在各处的关键技术知识。有些存在于员工的脑海中，有些则埋藏在旧邮件、Slack 聊天记录、支持工单和数据库中。公司之所以能够运转，是因为人们依稀记得这些知识的存放位置以及如何应用它们。 但人工智能代理不能这样运作。如果我们想让每家公司都实现人工智能自动化，我们需要一个新的基本要素：企业大脑。 我们需要盖瑞的 G-Brain，但要适用于全球所有企业。这套系统能够从所有这些分散的来源中提取知识，对其进行结构化处理，保持其时效性，并将其转化为人工智能可执行的技能文件。 这并非公司范围内的搜索，也不是基于文档的聊天机器人。它是一张公司运作方式的动态地图：退款如何处理、定价例外情况如何决定，以及工程师如何应对突发事件。 然后，人工智能系统就可以利用该技能文件来安全、一致地完成实际工作。 企业大脑成为原始企业数据和可靠的人工智能自动化之间的缺失环节。 我认为世界上每家公司都需要一个。 如果你正在开发这个项目，你应该申请加入 YC。

### Counter-Swarm Defense反集群防御

By [Tyler Bosmeny](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/tyler-bosmeny)  
作者： [泰勒·博斯梅尼](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/tyler-bosmeny)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O8JiNHcI3jI)

Last month, a swarm of cheap Iranian drones took out an AWS data center. Nobody stopped them. Now imagine an incoming swarm of a thousand coordinated drones. We'd stand no chance. While everyone is racing to stop individual drones, the next wave is coming and it's worse: not one drone, but swarms. Cheap, autonomous, jam-resistant, and lethal. A Patriot missile costs three million dollars. An FPV drone? Five hundred bucks. All of the cost advantage lies with the attackers. We are not ready. Today, counter-drone defense is a messy pile of radars, cameras, jammers, interceptors, humans with binoculars, and systems that don't talk to each other. That might work against a hobby drone. It will not work against a swarm. I want to fund founders building the counter-swarm stack. That could mean high-capacity interceptors — a single platform that neutralizes fifty drones, not one. Software that fuses every sensor and every defender on a site into a single real-time picture. We need non-kinetic defenses that don't exist yet: aerosols that foul rotors, streamers that entangle swarms. We need new attacks on the autonomy stack itself now that radio jamming is becoming obsolete. The key insight is drone defense is looking less and less like operating a weapon and more like running a real-time distributed system. The winning companies will look more like Cloudflare than Raytheon. If you're working on the future of drone defense, and how we can restore the cost advantage to defenders — we want to hear from you.  
上个月，一群廉价的伊朗无人机袭击了 AWS 的一个数据中心。没有人阻止它们。 现在想象一下，一千架协同作战的无人机蜂拥而至。我们毫无招架之力。当所有人都在争分夺秒地拦截零星无人机时，下一波攻击已经到来，而且更加可怕：不是一架无人机，而是无人机群。它们价格低廉、自主运行、抗干扰能力强，而且杀伤力惊人。 一枚“爱国者”导弹价值三百万美元。一架 FPV 无人机呢？只要五百美元。所有成本优势都在攻击者手中。我们还没做好准备。 如今的反无人机防御体系杂乱无章，雷达、摄像头、干扰器、拦截器、手持望远镜的人员以及彼此互不通信的系统混杂在一起。这或许对付业余无人机有效，但对付无人机群则毫无用处。 我想资助那些构建反集群技术的创始人。 这意味着我们需要高容量拦截器——一个能够拦截五十架无人机的单一平台，而不是仅仅一架。我们需要一种软件，能够将现场所有传感器和所有防御人员的信息融合到一个实时画面中。我们需要目前尚不存在的非动能防御手段：例如，能够污染旋翼的气溶胶，以及能够缠住无人机群的流光。随着无线电干扰技术的逐渐过时，我们需要针对无人机自主系统本身的新型攻击手段。 关键在于，无人机防御越来越不像操作武器，而更像是运行一个实时分布式系统。最终的赢家将更像 Cloudflare，而不是雷神公司。 如果您正在研究无人机防御的未来，以及如何恢复防御者的成本优势——我们很想听听您的意见。

### Dynamic Software Interfaces动态软件接口

By [Ankit Gupta](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/ankit-gupta)  
作者： [安基特·古普塔](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/ankit-gupta)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_2LcG-3zCdY)

Before AI, users of a piece of software all interacted with the same interface. There were only light customizations like a few different views or theme and color options. When users think about "personalization" like on Netflix, it still has the same layout for everyone, just different imagery. As a result, most software has a one-sized-fits-all feel rather than being hypercustomized to a user. As an example: the way I use an email is very different from how most college students use email, yet all email clients look basically the same. The exception is in enterprise software, where forward deployed engineers customize software for each customer to make it a great experience for them. We think that coding agents have now gotten good enough to allow users to become their own forward deployed engineers and more radically customize the software they consume. I'm imagining users designing widely different interfaces for their use cases — perhaps my email client looks more like a task list, and a students' looks more like an events calendar. But these two interfaces likely share some underlying primitives and design decisions that a software team can build and ship. We think that in the future, software companies will ship these shared primitives with full intention that users will heavily modify the final interfaces. To enable this future, we will have to rethink the whole stack of software delivery. How will a developer make software that can be accessed by the user's coding agents? Do they have to deliver source code rather than packaged binaries? Can they only modify front-end visual elements, or are there ways for them to modify middleware on the fly to enable more interesting use-cases? If you're a radical thinker looking to define the future of software, we'd love to hear from you.  
在人工智能出现之前，所有软件用户都使用相同的界面。只有一些简单的自定义选项，例如几种不同的视图或主题和颜色。即使用户想到像 Netflix 这样的“个性化”服务，其布局对每个人来说仍然相同，只是图像不同而已。因此，大多数软件给人的感觉是千篇一律，而不是根据用户进行高度定制。举个例子：我使用电子邮件的方式与大多数大学生截然不同，但所有电子邮件客户端的界面看起来都大同小异。 企业软件领域是个例外，前线部署的工程师会为每个客户定制软件，以创造良好的客户体验。 我们认为，如今的编码代理已经足够强大，能够让用户成为自己的前线工程师，并更彻底地定制他们使用的软件。我设想用户会根据自己的使用场景设计截然不同的界面——也许我的电子邮件客户端更像一个任务列表，而学生的客户端则更像一个活动日历。但这两个界面很可能共享一些底层基本组件和设计决策，软件团队可以构建并发布这些组件和决策。 我们认为，未来软件公司在发布这些共享的基本组件时，会完全抱着用户会大幅修改最终界面的意图。 为了实现这样的未来，我们需要重新思考整个软件交付流程。开发者如何才能开发出用户可以通过代码代理访问的软件？他们是否必须交付源代码而不是打包好的二进制文件？他们只能修改前端视觉元素，还是可以动态修改中间件以实现更多有趣的用例？如果您是一位具有创新思维、渴望定义软件未来的开发者，我们非常期待您的加入。

### Electronics in Space太空电子设备

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pAV8f4Snx7Q)

We are about to see an absolutely huge increase in the capacity that humanity has to put things in space because of reusable rockets from SpaceX and Stoke Space. This means that we're going to need enormous amounts of new compute capacity in space. The particular electronics in space we'd like to see is inference chips. There's going to be an absolutely enormous market for inference chips in space. What we mean by chips in space is something that is slightly optimized for mass, slightly optimized for thermal, and slightly optimized for radiation. If you are working at SpaceX or NVIDIA and you've been spending time doing chip design, then Y Combinator would love to hear from you.  
由于 SpaceX 和 Stokes Space 的可重复使用火箭，人类向太空输送货物的能力即将迎来巨大的飞跃。这意味着我们将需要大量的太空计算能力。 我们希望在太空中使用的特定电子设备是推理芯片。太空推理芯片的市场潜力巨大。我们所说的太空芯片是指在质量、热性能和辐射防护方面都经过优化的芯片。 如果你在 SpaceX 或 NVIDIA 工作，并且一直从事芯片设计，那么 Y Combinator 非常希望听到你的消息。

### Hardware Supply Chain硬件供应链

By [Nicolas Dessaigne](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/nicolas-dessaigne)  
作者： [尼古拉斯·德赛涅](https://www.ycombinator.com/people/nicolas-dessaigne)

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0gCeJFyRYx4)

We are funding more and more hardware companies, from medical devices to home robots to space companies. But building hardware in the US is still far too slow compared to China. In Shenzhen, a team can go from design to a new physical part in a day. In the US, that same loop often takes weeks. And that gap compounds. The problem isn't just the supply chain. It's iteration speed. China wins because hardware teams can move fast. They have dense supplier networks, rapid turnaround, and tight coordination between design and production. In the US, this system barely exists. A few startups are starting to build parts of it. For example, Hlabs (W26) is building actuators; Prototyping.io (P26) helps turn designs into mechanical parts in days. But the overall stack is still missing.
